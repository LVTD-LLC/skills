# Commitment and Consistency Knowledge

Source: Robert B. Cialdini, Influence: The Psychology of Persuasion, local text extraction in tmp/influence-analysis/influence.txt.

Source lines for this topic: 439-861.

## Overview

Design commitments that help users act on real intent, not commitments that trap them into defending a decision. The consistency drive is useful when it supports identity-aligned action and risky when it hides changing terms.

## Key Concepts

### Consistency drive

**Definition**: After a choice or stand, people feel pressure to act consistently with it. Cialdini introduces this mechanism at lines 439-520.

### Public commitment

**Definition**: Commitments become more durable when visible to others. The public-eye section starts at line 628.

### Effortful commitment

**Definition**: More effort can increase ownership and persistence; the effort section starts at line 654.

### Inner choice

**Definition**: Commitments are strongest when people experience them as freely chosen; the inner-choice section starts at line 698.

### Lowball

**Definition**: A choice is secured under attractive terms, then terms change after commitment. The notes cite controlled evidence at line 2196.

## How It Relates To Other Influence Principles

- Reciprocity asks whether value creates obligation.
- Commitment asks whether prior action creates consistency pressure.
- Social proof asks whether other people's behavior is being used as evidence.
- Liking asks whether relationship cues are affecting trust.
- Authority asks whether credentials or symbols create deference.
- Scarcity asks whether loss or limited availability creates urgency.
- Contrast asks whether sequence changes perceived value.

## Common Misconceptions

- **Myth**: Persuasion is unethical by default. **Reality**: Persuasion is useful when it gives truthful, decision-relevant evidence.
- **Myth**: If a cue works, it should be used. **Reality**: A cue should be used only when it accurately represents the situation.
- **Myth**: Removing pressure means weakening the message. **Reality**: Honest specificity often increases trust and long-term conversion quality.
